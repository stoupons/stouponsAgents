# Water Softener FAQ

## What does a water softener do?
A water softener removes the minerals that make water "hard", mainly calcium and magnesium. It uses a process called ion exchange: water flows through a tank of resin beads that trap hardness minerals and release a small amount of sodium in their place. Soft water prevents scale build-up in pipes, water heaters and fixtures, helps soap lather better, and reduces spots on dishes and glass.

## What water softener sizes does Stoupons offer?
Stoupons installs three water softener sizes: 48,000 grain (48K), 64,000 grain (64K) and 96,000 grain (96K). The right size depends on how many people live in the home, how hard the water is, and how much water the household uses. A Stoupons advisor or the recommendation assistant can suggest the best size for your home.

## How do I know which softener size I need?
A common sizing method is: number of people x about 75 gallons per person per day x water hardness in grains per gallon = grains removed per day. Multiply by 7 to get the weekly need, and choose a system whose capacity covers about one week between regenerations. As a rough guide, 48K suits 1-3 people, 64K suits 4-5 people, and 96K suits 6 or more people or very hard water.

## What is a softener loop and do I need one?
A softener loop is a section of plumbing, usually in the garage, built so that a water softener can be connected to the home's main water supply. Many newer homes have one. Stoupons installations normally connect to an existing loop. If your home has no loop, a specialist can assess whether one can be added; this is treated as a custom installation and is reviewed before it is quoted.

## How often do I need to add salt?
Check the brine tank about once a month and keep it at least one-third to one-half full of salt. Most households add salt every 1-2 months, depending on household size and water hardness.

## Is softened water safe to drink?
Yes. Softening adds a small amount of sodium to the water, roughly proportional to the hardness removed. People on a sodium-restricted diet may prefer a reverse osmosis (RO) drinking water system at the kitchen sink, which removes most of that sodium.

## Does a water softener remove chlorine, bacteria or iron?
A water softener is designed to remove hardness minerals. It can handle small amounts of dissolved iron, but it does not remove chlorine taste and odor or bacteria. Chlorine is usually addressed with a carbon filter, and drinking water quality is improved with a reverse osmosis system.

## How long does a water softener installation take?
A standard installation on an existing softener loop usually takes about 2-4 hours. Custom installations, such as adding a new loop, can take longer.
